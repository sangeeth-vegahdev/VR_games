## Inspiration

Reaching out and touching a light is about the simplest thing a hand can do, and hand tracking is the one thing a
controller cannot fake. We wanted a game you understand in one second and can pick up for two minutes in a chair.

## What it does

You sit on a rooftop at night, above a sleeping city, with a curved wall of 38 hanging Edison bulbs inside arm's reach.
One bulb lights up. Touch it before the ring closes on it. Every bulb you hit sends power into the city, and its windows
come on with your score. Miss, and the city browns out. Lose every heart, and it goes dark.

- Cyan bulbs: touch fast. Faster touches score more, and hits in a row build a multiplier up to x5.
- Gold star bulbs: triple points.
- Red X bulbs: never touch.
- Lightning pairs: two bulbs joined by an arc. Touch both close together for double.
- SURGE: fill the meter, then clap your hands. The whole wall comes alive for 8 seconds at double points, and nothing can hurt you.
- A generated soundtrack adds layers as you climb, and each streak climbs a melody on top of it.
- Your lifetime score earns a rank and keeps part of the city lit between sessions.

## Hands first

The whole game, from the first menu to Exit, can be played with bare hands and no controller.

- Just point at a bulb: rest your hand ray on it for a fraction of a second and it lights up. Or poke it with a fingertip, aim a hand ray and pinch, or on Meta VR Glasses look and pinch.
- Clapping is the signature move, and the SURGE button is always there as an alternative for anyone using one hand.
- Controllers, a mouse and a touch screen also work, and none of them is required.

## Built for a seated session

- Everything sits within a seated reach. Nothing needs standing up or walking.
- The first round is a short tutorial where nothing can hurt you, so the game teaches itself in under a minute.
- Rounds are short. Taking the headset off, opening the system menu or dropping your hands out of view pauses cleanly,
  and play resumes after a short countdown.
- The game is about [size] MB including the SDK, with almost nothing to download beyond the code: it draws its world,
  and generates its music and sound, at run time.

## How we built it

- Meta Immersive Web SDK (IWSDK) on Three.js and WebXR, with hand tracking and gaze-and-pinch.
- Every bulb is one ECS entity that is both ray- and poke-interactable, so all input methods share one code path and a
  press is handled the moment the SDK reports it.
- All 38 bulbs are drawn with five instanced meshes, so they cost five draw calls.
- Web Audio only: a small sequencer for the music, and HRTF 3D sound, so you can hear which side a bulb lit on.
- The layout was measured against the Meta VR Glasses view (37 degrees sideways, 25 up, 43 down) by an automated test.
- Comfort and safety: the world never moves around the player, and nothing flashes more than three times a second.

## Challenges

- Hands reaching across a wall of bulbs brush the wrong ones. A brush now costs your streak, not a heart.
- Fitting a board you can reach into the Glasses' narrower view, without making the bulbs too small to touch.
- Keeping a rich-looking scene cheap: instancing, unlit materials and redrawing text only when it changes.
